# Three-city accessibility and relative-price transfer research handover

Generated by `python -m src.reporting.study_report` from immutable authoritative PostGIS packages and verified saved predictions. Copenhagen means the combined Copenhagen–Frederiksberg study area.

**Release status: PARTIAL / BLOCKED.** All three cities’ primary predictive ladders and all six directional transfer experiments completed. Independent registered robustness is accounted for below. Stockholm association inference and dependent association sensitivities remain blocked by the frozen 5% missing-predictor guard. The full requested study is therefore not completely released. No old numerical result, fitted model, prediction, feature table or result prose substitutes for a fresh result.

## Scientific design and questions

1. How do Euclidean and walking-network measures characterize urban accessibility
   differently across Copenhagen, Oslo and Stockholm?
2. How much predictive information do Euclidean and walking-network accessibility
   measures add to Airbnb asking-price models within each city?
3. How robust are the predictive gains from accessibility when models are
   evaluated in geographically unseen neighbourhoods, and how consistent are
   those gains across Copenhagen, Oslo and Stockholm?
4. To what extent do Airbnb pricing relationships learned in one city transfer
   to another city, and does accessibility improve this transferability?

The final design was amended after historical results and feasibility evidence had been observed. It is not retrospectively preregistered. M0=X; ME=X+E; MW=X+W; MEW=X+E+W. X contains the common dwelling, host, amenity, native density and civic-distance controls. Four models share rows, observed outcomes and folds within each city. Within-city outcomes are log original observed positive native nightly asking prices.

## Limitations and claim discipline

| issue | consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Observed asking prices | Not transaction revenue, booked rates, causal willingness-to-pay or a temporal panel. |
| Missing observed prices | Strong source attrition; missing outcomes are never imputed. Price-reference and model samples differ transparently. |
| Stockholm inferential gate | Common complete-case association sample exists, but >5% union missingness blocks coefficients and dependent inference. |
| Operational geography | Provider folds and native population units have documented footprint/period differences; no exact official-boundary certification. |
| Context vintages | Native population periods differ; Stockholm gross area is rounded and historically derived; direct matching statistics avoid fabricated allocation. |
| OSM and coordinates | Completeness, map timing, displacement, connectors, component separation and finite extraction boundaries constrain measurement. |
| Validation uncertainty | Few geographic groups, overlapping training sets and spatial dependence; stability ranges do not establish independence or equivalence. |
| Relative transfer | Target price benchmark is observed; results measure structural relative-price transfer, not unseen-city absolute-price deployment. |
| External validity | Three capitals do not represent every Nordic or European city; network superiority is not assumed. |
